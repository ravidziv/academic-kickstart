# Recent publications audit — August 26, 2026

## Scope

Two official arXiv author queries returned 38 unique records first submitted in 2024–2026, including 26 first submitted in 2025–2026. All 26 now have site entries. The audit also checked recent conference publications with older preprints. This is a metadata and abstract review, not a full technical peer review or an exhaustive bibliography.

Conference status was checked against official proceedings, conference/workshop programs, or the author-supplied acceptance note on arXiv. No acceptance was inferred from an under-review submission. Original release dates were retained; the AISTATS entry uses its proceedings publication date.

## Homepage selection

1. Layer by Layer — retained first at the author’s request; ICML 2025 oral status verified.
2. Min-p — retained second at the author’s request.
3. S-JEPA — predictive speech representations; also retained as the first applied-work example.
4. On Training in Imagination — a direct connection to learned world models and policy optimization.
5. You Had One Job — task-aware quantization connects hidden representations to memory and inference costs.
6. Attention Sinks and Compression Valleys — the connection between representation compression and computation across layers.

The remaining papers are in the full archive. Recent Publications displays the newest non-featured entries: DRY, XTC, and HP-JEPA. The earlier GMM-Anchored JEPA study is cross-linked with S-JEPA, without treating the two as independent headline examples.

## Editorial distinctions

Task-Aware Quantization, NdLinear, and Inheritune concern model size or execution efficiency. Attention Sinks and From Tokens to Thoughts study compression of representations; they should not be described as deployable model-compression algorithms. S-JEPA provides predictive representations for speech, whereas On Training in Imagination directly analyzes training in learned dynamics and reward models.

World models, memory, and continual learning remain research interests confirmed by the author. The memory essay concerns state within a sequence, and the recent-paper audit does not by itself establish a deployed continual-learning or personalization system.
